Does PHQ-9 improve prediction?

Cross-validated, survey-weighted AUC (5 folds repeated 3 times). An AUC of 0.5 is a coin flip and 1.0 is perfect ranking. A change near zero means PHQ-9 adds almost nothing once age, BMI and the other answers are known. For one person, PHQ-9 can still shift the estimate (see the change shown on each card above), yet it barely improves how well the models rank people.

Precision, recall and other classification measures

Each cell shows the value without PHQ-9 / with PHQ-9, from the same cross-validation, survey-weighted. A person is counted as a predicted case when the predicted risk is at or above the average risk of the training group (the weighted prevalence). Recall (also called sensitivity) is the share of people who have the condition that the model finds. Precision is the share of flagged people who truly have it. Specificity is the share of people without the condition correctly left unflagged. F1 combines precision and recall. Because diabetes and high blood pressure are not the majority outcome, accuracy alone can look good even for a weak model, so read it together with recall and precision.

What the models adjust for

These ten answers are included in every model, with and without PHQ-9, so any effect of PHQ-9 is judged after they are taken into account.

Demographic. Age, sex, race and ethnicity.

Socioeconomic. Education, income compared with the poverty line.

Lifestyle. Physical activity, smoking, alcohol use, sleep hours.

Body. Body mass index (BMI).

About this tool

Research demonstration, not medical advice. It cannot diagnose anything or guide treatment. Talk to a clinician about your own health.

Data. adults aged 18 and over with a PHQ-9 score, from the US National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey. Models were fitted with survey weights, so they describe the US adult population.

Models. Logistic regression, a shallow decision tree, a random forest and gradient boosting, all fitted with scikit-learn and run in this page.

Read with care. The data are a single snapshot, so associations are not causes. The percentages are what the models estimate for people with these answers, not a personal probability.
